# Exceedance Ratio Threshold

**Parameter:** `exceedance_ratio_threshold` (FRF) /
`psd_exceedance_ratio_threshold` (PSD)
**Default:** `0.7`
**Range:** `0.2 – 0.99`

## What it is

The minimum fraction of samples (0 to 1) that must exceed the dynamic
threshold before the Lowpass method flags the signal as BAD.

## What it does

```
exceedance_count = sum(|residual[i]| > dynamic_threshold for i in range(N))
exceedance_ratio = exceedance_count / N

lowpass_is_bad   = exceedance_ratio > exceedance_ratio_threshold
```

So if `0.7` (70%) or more of all samples have residuals larger than
`dynamic_threshold`, it's flagged BAD.

## Why the default is 0.7 (intentionally permissive)

`0.7` is deliberately high — requiring **70%** of the signal to be "rough"
before flagging BAD. This means the LP method, at default settings, only
fires on **pervasively rough** signals: double-hits that rattle across the
whole response window, overload-clipped traces, broken cables.

The permissive default reflects how `relative_residual_ratio` is defined:
even clean signals routinely produce exceedance ratios in the 0.3–0.6 range
because "10% of max" is very small when max is small. Setting the threshold
lower would flag virtually everything clean.

## Effect of changing it

- **Lower threshold** (e.g. 0.7 → 0.3) → LP method is more sensitive →
  signals with even partial roughness get flagged BAD.
- **Higher threshold** (e.g. 0.7 → 0.9) → LP method is stricter → only the
  roughest signals fail.

If you want the LP method to detect localised spike-type bad hits rather
than pervasive roughness, lower this threshold **and** lower
`relative_residual_ratio` in tandem — both are needed.

## Calibration

All three estimators target this parameter:

- **Percentile:** midpoint or 95th percentile rule on the Good/Bad
  populations' `exceedance_ratio` values.
- **Bayesian:** grid search over `[0.05, 0.99]`, 300 grid points, sigmoid
  likelihood.
- **ROC/Youden:** threshold maximising Youden's J on the labelled data.
