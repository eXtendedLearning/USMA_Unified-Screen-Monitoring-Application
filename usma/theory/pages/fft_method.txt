# FFT Method — Overview

Source: `usma/analysis/signal.py`, inside `_analyze_wave_pattern()`.

The FFT method is one of the two independent analyses USMA runs on every
FRF/PSD ROI. It answers a single, structural question: **how much of this
signal's energy sits at high spatial frequencies?**

## What it operates on

The **pixel-space signal** (raw column positions of the detected colour
trace), not the physical-unit signal. The reason is that the frequency content
of interest is a structural property of the *shape* of the signal, independent
of what physical units it's in.

This means the FFT method is immune to Y-axis miscalibration: regardless of
whether `y_axis_min/max` is set correctly, the FFT shape-analysis is the same.

## The steps

```
1. Detrend: subtract the mean from signal_pixels
2. Apply rfft() -> complex spectrum
3. Compute magnitude spectrum: fft_mags = abs(yf)
4. Build normalised frequency axis with rfftfreq(N, 1) -> values 0 to 0.5 (Nyquist)
5. Compute high_freq_energy = sum of fft_mags**2 above fft_cutoff_frequency
6. Compute energy_ratio = high_freq_energy / total_energy
7. Flag: fft_is_bad = energy_ratio > fft_energy_ratio_threshold
```

The signal is **not** sampled in time — it's sampled in pixels horizontally
across the ROI. So frequencies produced by the FFT are **spatial**
frequencies, in cycles/pixel.

## Intuition

A clean, smooth FRF/PSD signal changes slowly across the ROI, so most of its
energy sits at low spatial frequencies. A noisy or double-hit signal has rapid
oscillations, elevating high-frequency energy. The FFT method measures the
ratio of high-frequency energy to total energy and flags the signal as BAD
once it exceeds a threshold.

## What the FFT method does NOT do

- It does not operate on the physical signal. The result is identical for
  miscalibrated Y-axes.
- It does not model noise separately from the signal — everything above the
  cutoff is treated as "high-frequency energy".
- It does not depend on the Lowpass method in any way. Both methods vote
  independently and feed into the classifier.

The two tunable parameters live on their own pages: `fft_cutoff_frequency`
(where the low/high boundary sits) and `fft_energy_ratio_threshold` (the
pass/fail cutoff on the resulting ratio).
