# Relative Residual Ratio

**Parameter:** `relative_residual_ratio` (FRF) /
`psd_relative_residual_ratio` (PSD)
**Default:** `0.10` (i.e. 10% of the peak residual)
**Range:** `0.02 – 0.50`

## What it is

A **dimensionless fraction** in `[0, 1]` (displayed as a ratio in the UI). It
defines the exceedance threshold as a proportion of the peak residual
amplitude.

## What it does

```
max_abs_residual  = max(|residual|)
dynamic_threshold = relative_residual_ratio * max_abs_residual
exceedance[i]     = |residual[i]| > dynamic_threshold
```

A residual sample is counted as an "exceedance" if its absolute value
exceeds this fraction of the peak residual.

The default `0.10` means: **a sample is an exceedance if it exceeds 10% of
the largest residual in the signal**.

## Intuition (read carefully)

- **Clean signal:** residuals are uniformly small. `max` is small.
  `10% of max` is very small. Almost all samples exceed it.
  `exceedance_ratio ≈ 1.0`. **But this is not flagged** because the
  `exceedance_ratio_threshold` (default 0.7) is *also* high — both numbers
  are meant to be interpreted as "proportion of samples that are
  proportionally close to peak".

- **Double-hit / spiky signal:** residuals have large localised spikes
  mixed with calm regions. `max` is large (set by the spike). `10% of max`
  is moderate. Many calm-region samples fall below it.
  `exceedance_ratio < 0.7`. Also not flagged.

- **Pervasively rough signal:** `max` is high AND most samples are also
  high. Most samples exceed 10% of max → `exceedance_ratio > 0.7` →
  **flagged BAD**.

So what this pair of thresholds *really* detects is **pervasive roughness**,
not isolated spikes or uniform cleanness. That is the design intent.

## Dimensionless and scale-invariant

Because the threshold is a fraction of the signal's own peak residual, the
LP method works the same way regardless of:

- Signal amplitude range
- Physical units (g/N, m/s², dimensionless)
- Y-axis calibration correctness

This is why the LP method is reliable on pixel-space signals without any
user-supplied calibration of the Y-axis.

## Effect of changing it

- **Lower ratio** (e.g. 0.10 → 0.05) → lower dynamic threshold → more
  exceedances → **easier to flag BAD**.
- **Higher ratio** (e.g. 0.10 → 0.20) → higher dynamic threshold → fewer
  exceedances → **harder to flag**.

## Working with `exceedance_ratio_threshold`

The two knobs are **complementary**. Lower the ratio to find more
exceedances; lower the exceedance threshold to accept fewer of them as
"rough enough to fail". In practice, tune `relative_residual_ratio` first
to shape how residuals are counted, then tune
`exceedance_ratio_threshold` to set the pass/fail line.

## Calibration

All three estimators target this parameter:

- **Percentile:** sweeps candidate ratios over `[0.02, 0.50]` in steps of
  0.01 and picks the one with the highest Good/Bad separation.
- **Bayesian:** grid search over `[0.02, 0.50]`, 300 grid points, sigmoid
  likelihood.
- **ROC/Youden:** nested sweep — for each candidate ratio, recompute
  `exceedance_ratio` for every signal, then find the Youden-J-optimal
  threshold. The ratio producing the highest J wins.
