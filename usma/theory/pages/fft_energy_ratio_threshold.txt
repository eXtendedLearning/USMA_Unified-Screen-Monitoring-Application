# FFT Energy Ratio Threshold

**Parameter:** `fft_energy_ratio_threshold` (FRF) /
`psd_fft_energy_ratio_threshold` (PSD)
**Default:** `0.006`
**Range:** `0.0001 – 0.1` (practical)

## What it is

The minimum `energy_ratio` to flag a signal as high-frequency. Dimensionless,
ranges roughly `0.001 – 0.1` on real data.

`energy_ratio` is defined (see `fft_cutoff_frequency`) as:

```
energy_ratio = high_freq_energy / total_energy
```

…i.e. the fraction of total spectral power sitting above the cutoff.

## What it does

```
fft_is_bad = energy_ratio > fft_energy_ratio_threshold
```

If the fraction of total spectral power above the cutoff exceeds this
threshold, the FFT method votes BAD.

## Why the default is 0.006

0.006 means: if more than 0.6% of the signal's total power is in high spatial
frequencies, flag it. This is **intentionally sensitive** — a handful of
pixel-width ripples typically produce energy ratios in the 0.01–0.05 range, so
anything even mildly noisy will cross this bar.

The default reflects the typical use case: clean FRF/PSD plots from a
well-coupled impact test. If your baseline measurements are inherently
noisier (weak signal-to-noise, rough instrumentation), you will need to raise
the threshold or the FFT method will flag everything.

## Effect of changing it

- **Lower threshold** → FFT method is more sensitive → more signals flagged
  BAD by the FFT vote.
- **Higher threshold** → fewer signals flagged → FFT vote only fires on very
  noisy signals.

## Calibration

All three estimators target this parameter:

- **Percentile:** midpoint between `max(good_values)` and `min(bad_values)`
  if cleanly separated; else 95th percentile of GOOD.
- **Bayesian:** grid search over `[0.0005, 0.15]` with a sigmoid likelihood,
  300 grid points.
- **ROC/Youden:** threshold maximising Youden's J on the labelled data.
