# LP Cutoff

**Parameter:** `lowpass_cutoff` (FRF) / `psd_lowpass_cutoff` (PSD)
**Default:** `0.07`
**Range:** `0.03 – 0.2`

## What it is

The **normalised cutoff frequency** of the Butterworth lowpass filter used to
extract a smooth envelope from the pixel-space signal. The normalisation is
the same as the FFT method's: 0 to 1.0, where 1.0 = Nyquist. In practice the
useful range stays below ~0.4.

## What it does

Everything **below** this frequency passes through the filter unchanged.
Everything **above** it is attenuated.

- The **filtered** signal is the "smooth envelope" — what the signal looks
  like once its rapid oscillations are removed.
- The **residual** = signal − filtered = what was left over after smoothing.

```
filtered         = sosfiltfilt(butter(order, cutoff, 'low', output='sos'),
                               signal_detrended)
residual         = signal_detrended - filtered
max_abs_residual = max(|residual|)
```

## Intuition

Think of the lowpass filter as drawing a smooth curve through the signal.
The residual is the jagged deviation between the actual signal and that
smooth curve.

- A **good hit** has tiny, uniformly distributed residuals (the signal *is*
  smooth).
- A **bad hit** — rattling, double-hit, overload — has large, non-uniform
  residuals.

## Effect of changing it

- **Lower cutoff** → tighter smoothing → more of the signal ends up in the
  residual → more exceedances → **easier to flag BAD**.
- **Higher cutoff** → looser smoothing → only very rapid oscillations end up
  in the residual → fewer exceedances → **harder to flag**.

## Why 0.07

The default matches the FFT cutoff default — but this is **coincidence, not
coupling**. The two parameters are independently tuned and independently
calibrated.

## Calibration

Only the `PercentileBoundaryEstimator` sweeps this parameter. Candidates are
tested over `[0.03, 0.20]` in steps of 0.01. For each candidate, it re-runs
the LP residual computation on pixel-space signals and picks the cutoff that
maximises mean separation between Good and Bad `exceedance_ratio` values.
